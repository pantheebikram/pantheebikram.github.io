01 · About

Engineering the grid with math you can trust

I am a PhD researcher in the Department of Electrical & Biomedical Engineering at the University of Vermont, advised by Dr. Amritanshu Pandey. I grew up in Nepal, where an unreliable grid is a fact of everyday life, and that is part of why I care about getting power-system analysis right.

Modern distribution grids are unbalanced, three-phase, and full of new loads such as EV chargers, so the optimization problems that describe them are nonconvex and hard. Off-the-shelf solvers often return a local solution with no guarantee of quality. My work reformulates these problems as bilinear and mixed-integer bilinear programs and solves them with spatial branch-and-bound enhanced by bound tightening and presolve, so the answer comes with a certificate: a gap that is effectively zero.

I apply these methods to the problems utilities and planners care about: Is this grid state even feasible? Where is it failing? What is the true state of the network? Where should we build EV chargers so the grid can carry them?

Open to collaborations with utilities, grid-software companies, and national labs on research internships, industry R&D, and optimization-at-scale problems. Get in touch →

02 · Research

Three threads, one toolkit

Each problem is a hard nonconvex program. The common thread is a pipeline that turns it into one we can solve to certified global optimality.

Three-phase AC infeasibility analysis

When a grid's power-flow equations have no solution, operators need to know where and by how much. I reformulate the nonconvex infeasibility problem as a bilinear program and solve it with spatial B&B and bound tightening, scaling to 5,000+ node networks with gap < 10⁻⁴.

HICSS-59 · arXiv:2508.15937

Distribution grid state estimation

State estimation recovers voltages and flows from noisy, sparse measurements. Local solvers can settle on the wrong state. I extend the global-optimization pipeline to three-phase distribution state estimation and solve it to a near-zero optimality gap.

Working paper · 2026

Grid-aware EV charging placement

Grid-ECO places EV charging stations inside a distribution feeder using census-level demand, solving a mixed-integer bilinear program to a 0.00% gap on Seattle data (max 753 s), well ahead of commercial solvers. The related work in Transportation Research Part D adds equity and cost.

arXiv:2602.12473 · TR Part D 2026

Find the global minimum, with proof

A local solver can get stuck in any valley. Branch-and-bound splits the domain, builds a convex lower bound on each piece, and prunes pieces that cannot beat the best solution found so far. When the bounds meet, the solution is certified.
